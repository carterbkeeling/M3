The idea

Swap, don't count.

Every module is swapped at every restock, however much was used.

It's the Airplane Food Model: airline catering carts are swapped whole at every turnaround, no matter how much was eaten.

How it works

Load. Swap. Count.

  1. 01 · At the warehouse

    Load

    Each module is filled with a set quantity, closed, and labeled with a placard naming the product inside.

  2. 02 · On site

    Swap

    Full modules go in. Used modules go back to the vendor.

  3. 03 · Back at the warehouse

    Count

    Returned modules are counted in a controlled environment. RMAs and invoices are issued as needed.

    • RMA
    • Invoice

    Counted here, not on site.

Modular by design

Any module. Any slot.

Every slot has the same universal fitment, so modules (modular shelves) plug into any slot.

Unloading and restocking take minimal technical expertise or troubleshooting.

  • Universal fitment The same fitment in every slot, with a low-voltage connector
  • Module A closed unit with its own door, placard and lock
  • Body Injection-molded plastic

Connected, not counting

Connected. Not counting.

The internet connection does three things.

  1. 01 · Access control Tap a badge or phone at the reader to unlock a door. Every opening is logged.
  2. 02 · Plugged-in status Each slot reports whether a module is plugged in.
  3. 03 · Service and restock alerts Restock alerts follow your restock schedule. Service alerts flag fitment or lock faults.

m³ doesn't count items. Consumption is determined back at your warehouse.

Background

Why m³ exists.

In vendor-managed inventory (VMI), the vendor keeps a site stocked with PPE and MRO supplies like gloves, glasses and batteries. It restocks on a schedule and bills for what was used.

That usually means counting on site, item by item, at every visit, and troubleshooting machines along the way. m³ takes the count off site. Modules are swapped whole and counted back at the warehouse.

The idea comes from airline catering.
